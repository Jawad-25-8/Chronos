MESOAMERICA · ANCIENT WORLD

THE MAYA

A remarkable civilization of city-states, monumental temples, sophisticated calendars, mathematics, and writing that flourished across Mesoamerica.

REGION

Southern Mexico & Central America

EARLY DEVELOPMENT

c. 2000 BCE onward

FAMOUS FOR

Temples & Calendars

WRITING

Maya Hieroglyphs

01 / OVERVIEW

A World of Cities in the Jungle

The Maya developed across parts of present-day Mexico, Guatemala, Belize, Honduras, and El Salvador. Their history spans thousands of years, and Maya communities continue to live in the region today.

Unlike empires governed by one permanent central ruler, many ancient Maya kingdoms operated as independent city-states. Their rulers competed, formed alliances, fought wars, and sponsored impressive ceremonial architecture.

Maya achievements included a sophisticated writing system, advanced mathematics, astronomical observations, and interlocking calendar systems. Their history was diverse, changing across regions and centuries.

02 / HISTORICAL PERIODS

The Long Maya Story

c. 2000 BCE – 1000 BCE

Early Maya Communities

Farming villages grew around crops such as maize, beans, and squash. Communities developed regional traditions that helped shape later Maya society.

c. 1000 BCE – 250 CE

Preclassic Period

Settlements expanded, political centers emerged, and monumental architecture became increasingly important in several Maya regions.

c. 250 – 900 CE

Classic Period

Many Maya city-states reached great prominence. Rulers commissioned temples and inscriptions, while scribes recorded dynasties, events, and religious ideas.

c. 900 – 1500s CE

Postclassic Period

Political centers shifted and new powers rose. Trade, regional alliances, and cultural traditions continued, with different developments in different areas.

03 / CITIES AND ARCHITECTURE

Stone Cities Beneath the Sky

Maya cities contained plazas, palaces, temples, ball courts, and carefully planned ceremonial spaces. Buildings were often decorated with carved stone, painted surfaces, and inscriptions.

GUATEMALA

Tikal

One of the great Classic-period Maya centers, known for its towering temple pyramids and extensive urban remains.

MEXICO

Chichén Itzá

A major city of the northern Maya lowlands, famous for monumental architecture and the pyramid commonly called El Castillo.

MEXICO

Palenque

A renowned Maya center celebrated for its inscriptions, sculptural art, and the tomb of the ruler K'inich Janaab' Pakal.

04 / SCIENCE AND KNOWLEDGE

Mathematics, Writing, and the Heavens

Maya scholars developed a place-value number system that used a symbol for zero. Their mathematical knowledge supported calendar calculations and astronomical record-keeping.

Maya hieroglyphic writing represented language through a combination of signs. Inscriptions on monuments, pottery, and other objects preserve information about rulers, political events, rituals, and historical dates.

Maya calendar systems tracked time in several ways. Astronomical observations helped scholars understand recurring celestial cycles, while calendars also organized ceremonies and everyday life.

05 / SOCIETY AND BELIEFS

A Connected Community

Maya society included rulers, nobles, priests, scribes, artisans, merchants, farmers, and laborers. Farming sustained cities and supported large building projects.

Religious life connected people to deities, ancestors, natural forces, and the cycles of the cosmos. Beliefs and practices varied over time and across communities. Ceremonies, offerings, and sacred places held important roles in public and private life.

Trade connected Maya communities through the exchange of goods such as obsidian, jade, salt, cacao, and crafted objects.

06 / THE LEGACY

The Maya Are Still Here.

Ancient Maya cities changed, and some major centers were abandoned, but Maya people did not disappear. Millions of Maya descendants live across Mesoamerica today, maintaining languages, cultural traditions, and community identities. Their history is both an ancient story and a living one.
