THE ANCIENT WORLD

THE ROMAN EMPIRE

From a small settlement on the banks of the Tiber to a Mediterranean superpower, Rome transformed warfare, government, architecture, law, and culture.

HEARTLAND

Italian Peninsula

IMPERIAL PERIOD

27 BCE – 476 CE*

LANGUAGES

Latin and Greek

KNOWN FOR

Legions and Engineering

01 / OVERVIEW

A City That Changed the World

Rome began as a settlement in central Italy and gradually grew into one of the most influential powers of the ancient Mediterranean. Roman expansion brought many different peoples, languages, religions, and traditions into contact.

Roman history passed through several political systems. The traditional founding date is 753 BCE. Rome was ruled by kings in its earliest period, became a republic around 509 BCE, and developed into an empire under Augustus, beginning in 27 BCE.

Roman influence can still be seen in legal traditions, languages derived from Latin, architecture, engineering, literature, and political thought.

02 / THE TIMELINE

Rise, Expansion, and Transformation

753 BCE — 509 BCE

The Roman Kingdom

According to Roman tradition, Rome was founded by Romulus. The early settlement grew through trade, farming, and contact with neighboring peoples.

509 BCE — 27 BCE

The Roman Republic

Rome developed a republican political system with elected magistrates and a powerful Senate. Military campaigns expanded Roman control across Italy and the Mediterranean.

27 BCE — 180 CE

The Imperial High Point

Augustus established the imperial system. Under later emperors, Roman territory reached its greatest extent under Trajan around 117 CE. The second century saw extensive building and trade.

180 CE — 284 CE

Growing Instability

Political struggles, civil wars, economic pressures, and military challenges repeatedly disrupted imperial stability.

284 CE — 395 CE

Reform and Division

Emperors introduced major administrative and military reforms. Constantinople became a new imperial capital, and the empire's eastern and western administrations increasingly followed separate paths.

395 CE — 476 CE*

The Western Empire

The western imperial government faced continuing political and military pressures. In 476 CE, the last western emperor, Romulus Augustulus, was deposed.

*476 CE marks the conventional end of the Western Roman Empire. The Eastern Roman Empire continued for nearly another thousand years.

03 / PEOPLE OF ROME

Emperors Who Shaped History

Roman emperors held enormous authority, although their power depended on political alliances, military support, and the cooperation of administrators and elites.

27 BCE — 14 CE

Augustus

Rome's first emperor. He consolidated power after civil war and established institutions that helped stabilize the imperial system.

98 — 117 CE

Trajan

Under Trajan, Roman territory reached its greatest geographical extent, including conquests in Dacia and campaigns in the eastern Mediterranean.

117 — 138 CE

Hadrian

Hadrian traveled extensively through the provinces and strengthened borders. Hadrian's Wall in Britain remains a famous monument of his reign.

161 — 180 CE

Marcus Aurelius

An emperor and Stoic philosopher whose surviving writings, known as Meditations, remain influential. His reign also faced wars and a major epidemic.

04 / LIFE AND SOCIETY

Life in the Roman World

Roman society was diverse and deeply unequal. Social status, wealth, gender, citizenship, and legal standing shaped people's opportunities and everyday experiences.

DAILY LIFE

City and Countryside

Cities contained markets, workshops, public baths, temples, and crowded apartment buildings. Most people across the empire lived outside major cities.

GOVERNMENT

Law and Citizenship

Roman citizenship brought particular legal rights and responsibilities. Citizenship gradually expanded and was extended to most free inhabitants of the empire in 212 CE.

ECONOMY

Trade and Agriculture

Agriculture was central to the economy. Roads, sea routes, ports, and a widely used coinage system supported trade across distant provinces.

RELIGION

A Changing Spiritual World

Roman religious life included traditional gods, local traditions, and imported cults. Christianity spread through the empire and eventually became its dominant imperial religion.

05 / INNOVATION

Engineering an Empire

Rome's infrastructure helped connect distant provinces, move armies, supply cities, and support public life. Roman builders adapted ideas from other cultures and developed techniques on an impressive scale.

INFRASTRUCTURE

Roads

An extensive road network connected settlements, military bases, ports, and administrative centers.

WATER SYSTEMS

Aqueducts

Carefully engineered channels carried water to cities, supplying fountains, baths, and some private buildings.

ARCHITECTURE

Concrete and Arches

Roman builders used arches, vaults, domes, and concrete to create durable bridges, public buildings, and monumental spaces.

PUBLIC LIFE

The Colosseum

Opened around 80 CE, this vast amphitheater hosted public spectacles and became one of the best-known symbols of ancient Rome.

06 / MILITARY POWER

The Roman Legions

The Roman military evolved over centuries. Legions were organized formations of Roman citizen soldiers, supported at different times by auxiliary troops, cavalry, fleets, and provincial recruits.

Training, discipline, logistics, roads, fortifications, and adaptable tactics helped Rome wage campaigns across varied landscapes. Military success was important, but diplomacy, alliances, resources, and political decisions also shaped the empire's fortunes.

07 / THE LEGACY

Rome Did Not Simply Disappear.

The western imperial government ended in the fifth century, but Roman institutions, ideas, languages, and architectural traditions continued. The Eastern Roman Empire, often called the Byzantine Empire by modern historians, preserved Roman imperial traditions until Constantinople fell in 1453.
